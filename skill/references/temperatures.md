- **Why a tyre or brake runs hot or cold.** Read the trend first: a temperature still climbing
  lap after lap is not settled; a steady one that sits well off the optimum is a balance or
  setup matter. Then where the heat builds:
  - Brake-led: the brake on that wheel is the hottest or rises most, and the tyre gains most
    of its heat under braking, in the heavy braking zones. Cause: braking load or brake bias;
    lockups make it worse.
  - Load-led: the tyre gains its heat off the brakes in long corners, mostly in corners that
    load that side (a left-side tyre in right-handers). Cause: cornering load, sliding or
    the line.
  - Traction-led: a rear gains most at full throttle out of slow corners. Cause: wheelspin.
  - Pressure/camber-led: one strip of the tread (inner or outer) is clearly hotter than the
    others on every lap, or the pressure is far from the others. A hot inner strip with
    plenty of camber points to camber; a hot middle strip to high pressure.
- Name the most likely cause and the place, in one or two short sentences. Say "probably"
  when the evidence only leans one way; don't invent a setup value. Give an action only if
  asked.
- "Is it settling?" needs the trend across laps, not the lap's figures.
